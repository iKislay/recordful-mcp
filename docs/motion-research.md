# Recordful Motion: research and plan (2026-11-14)

What motion graphics should mean for Recordful, what others ship, what people ask for, and what only Recordful can do. Sound effects and music are out of scope for now. Feeds Phase B of `AI-AGENT-TODO.md` (in the recordful-eco folder) and row 14 of `roadmap.md`.

## 1. Where we start

| Surface | Has today | Missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Web editor (`recordful-ee/lib/edit.ts`, exported in the browser with mediabunny in `lib/render-edit.ts`) | Background, frame, zooms, camera layouts, captions (none/fade/rise/pop), click effects, watermark | **No text, title cards, shapes, arrows, blur or highlight boxes at all** |
| Desktop editor (`recordful-app/src/components/video-editor/types.ts`) | Annotations: `text`, `image`, `figure`, `blur` | Animated text, title cards, keyframes |
| Recording data we already keep | `Click.box`: the clicked control's bounds (`lib/edit.ts` `Click`), the pointer path, markers, the agent's own steps (navigate/click/type) over the bridge, DOM screenshots (extension 4.6.26) | Nothing stores the page's DOM, styles or text over time |

Cap is ahead of our web editor here: its project model (`crates/project/src/configuration.rs`) has `TextSegment` (animated text with gradient, glow, stroke, split layouts beside the screen), `MaskSegment` (`sensitive` blur or `highlight`, keyframed position, size and strength), `SceneSegment` (split layouts), 3D camera poses and keyboard-shortcut overlays.

## 3. What people actually ask for

Social search coverage is thin: web search doesn't reach Reddit or X threads directly, so much of the evidence below is from vendor blogs. The strongest first-hand evidence is Screen Studio's public request board.

1. **Text in screen recordings, first and above all.** Screen Studio's "Full text slides" request has **483 upvotes**, has said "Planned" since Nov 2022, and has 19 comments. People want to "publish fully finished videos from Screen Studio alone" instead of making title frames in Canva and re-editing elsewhere; want text *beside a highlight*, not only full-screen; and want to add a note after recording "when I forget to say something". "Annotation Tools" (arrows, lines, shapes, text) has **251 upvotes**, also Planned, since Mar 2024. Their reasons: viewers watch muted, and need to know where to look ("really crucial"). These are basic, and nobody in the screen-recorder category has shipped them well except Cap.
2. **AI motion graphics that don't look like AI.** It's a recurring complaint ([snapcn](https://snapcn.dev/why-ai-motion-graphics-look-the-same), [autoae](https://autoae.online/blog/ai-video-agents-motion-graphics-problem-2026), [digicurator](https://blog.digicuratoragency.com/ai-motion-graphics-skills-remotion-claude-code/)): a coding agent's launch video is "a centred headline on a purple-to-blue gradient", with pointless zooms and fake screen demos. The given causes are vague briefs, animating properties instead of planning shots, and **the agent never watching its own render**. The fixes offered are real footage of the product, shot lists, curated templates the agent picks from, and rendering stills to look at before finishing. HeyGen's own research says the same: guardrails made Remotion output "safer and more repetitive", and plain HTML restored variety.
3. **Four moments decide retention** (autoae's test of 50+ AI-agent videos): the hook frame, the topic banner or lower third, the **data callout** (agents said "73%" out loud and drew nothing), and the end card.
4. **Demos go stale.** A founder on Product Hunt "scrapped entire demos because a button moved or a flow changed". Teams keep inventories of which video shows which screen so they know what to re-record. Cloudflare's docs team is building internal tooling for code-driven screen demos so they can be rebuilt after a release. Nobody sells a fix for video.
5. **Developers want agents to make video.** Remotion's Agent Skills (>126k installs), HyperFrames' ~84k stars, and Anthropic, Tella, Figma and HeyGen all shipping motion features in 2026 show the demand. Most of it is standalone clips, though, not graphics inside a real product recording.

## 4. What only Recordful can do (nobody ships these)

Tella times graphics to **what you said**. Recordful is the only one that also knows **what you did**: it drives and watches the page it records. That is the moat. Each idea below is buildable with data we have or can capture in the extension.

| # | Idea | Why it's new | Built from | Size |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | **Action-timed graphics.** Callouts, lower thirds and spotlights attach to *events* (this click, this page, this typed field), not timestamps. "Opened Settings → Billing" becomes a lower third at that navigation; the clicked button gets a label pinned to its `Click.box` that rides along with the zoom | Tella has speech timing only; Claude Motion has no timing at all | `Click.box`, pointer path, agent steps and markers, page title and URL per navigation (to add) | Mid |
| B | **Element lift / spotlight.** At a click, the clicked control is cut out of the frame using `Click.box`, lifted with a shadow, scaled 1.2× while the rest dims and blurs, then set back. It's the Apple-keynote move, made automatically from real footage | Screen Studio and Cap zoom the whole frame; nobody lifts the element | `Click.box` + our canvas renderer | Small to mid |
| C | **Privacy masks from the DOM, not OCR.** While recording, the content script records the on-screen boxes of emails, API keys, card fields and `input[type=password]` (and anything the user tags), following scroll. Blur boxes come out placed and keyframed with no OCR. | Cap's masks are manual; Screen Studio's community tool uses OCR (misses things, guesses) | Content script (it already tracks clicks) + Cap-style `MaskSegment` | Mid |
| D | **Brand from the page.** The first time you record a site, read its computed styles: font families, primary button colour, background, logo or favicon. Every title, callout and end card then uses the product's own brand by default. This directly answers the "purple gradient" complaint | Others ask you to fill in a brand kit by hand | Content script reading `getComputedStyle` on the armed tab | Small |
| E | **Re-shoot when the UI changes ("living demos").** An agent-made recording keeps its script: the bridge steps (navigate/click/type), markers, and graphics attached to steps (idea A). "Re-shoot" replays the script on today's UI and rebuilds the same edit: zooms, callouts, captions, cuts. A step that no longer matches (a button moved) is flagged for a human | Arcade fixes text on still HTML steps only; nobody does this for video. It answers pain #4 directly | Agent bridge (exists), step-anchored edit (A), `edit_video` from the roadmap | Large; this is the headline |
| F | **The agent watches its own render.** Every motion tool returns a contact sheet of frames at its key moments, so the agent fixes "headline too small" before saying it's done | Tella exposes frames; nobody makes looking part of every edit | Roadmap gap #2 (frames/storyboard) | Small, once #2 exists |
| G | **Callouts for numbers.** When the transcript says a number or a percentage, the agent proposes a data callout at that word | Hits autoae's missed "data callout" moment | Transcript words (Deepgram already returns them) + the motion doc | Small |
| H | **Vertical cut that follows the action.** A 9:16 export pans to where the clicks and typing happen, instead of a fixed centre crop | Auto-reframe tools follow faces, not UI actions | Pointer path + `Click.box`, same maths as auto-zoom | Mid |
| I (later) | **Sharp zoom holds.** When a zoom holds on a still page, swap in a DOM screenshot of that moment so text stays crisp at 2× (Screen Studio lists "sharper text" on its roadmap) | Pixel zoom blurs text | DOM screenshot code (4.6.26) taken at click time | Mid; only works on still moments |

## 5. How to build it: three layers

**Layer 1: overlays in our own renderer (no AI, no credits, web and desktop).** Text and title cards (with in/out animations: fade, rise, pop, typewriter, by word), shapes and arrows, blur and highlight masks with keyframes, keystroke overlays, and B (element lift). They live in `Edit` beside `captions`, drawn by the same canvas code the browser export already uses, so preview and export match by construction. Copy the shapes of Cap's `TextSegment` / `MaskSegment` and the desktop app's annotation types, so the web and desktop editors share one model. This alone answers Screen Studio's top two requests (734 votes between them) and catches up with Cap.

**Layer 2: Recordful Motion documents (the agent's medium).** A constrained JSON scene graph (nodes: text, shape, image, the screen itself; keyframes with easing on any property; text animators; a fixed effect list), drawn by the same renderer. The agent edits it through commands, the way Tella does, never by writing raw code. That gives editable layers, a deterministic in-browser export, no sandbox to escape, and cheap tokens. The tools are `create_motion`, `apply_motion_commands`, `get_motion_schema`, `inspect_motion` and `motion_frames` (F). They are the same tools for the in-editor Agent and for `recordful-mcp`. Graphics anchor to transcript words (like Tella) **and** to recorded actions (A), which Tella can't do. Templates matter more than generation (finding §3.2): ship 20 to 30 hand-made templates (lower thirds, title cards, number callouts, end cards, chapter cards) with the brand from D, and have the agent fill and adjust them.

**Layer 3: HyperFrames for open-ended clips.** Full-frame intros, explainers, charts and 3D: anything the JSON graph can't express.
- The license allows it (Apache-2.0).
- **Now, with no server:** `recordful-mcp` gains `add_clip` (upload an MP4 into a video at a time). An agent with the HyperFrames skills renders on the user's own machine (Node 22 + FFmpeg) and inserts the clip. This costs us nothing and fits the "no hosted MCP" decision.
- **Later, in the product:** preview with `<hyperframes-player>` in a sandboxed iframe. Rendering needs headless Chrome + FFmpeg, which Vercel functions can't run. It would need a render worker (HyperFrames' AWS Lambda package, or a container) with a cache keyed by the HTML's hash, and credits.
- Avoid Remotion (company license above 3 people) and Diffusion Studio (non-commercial).

## 6. Suggested order

1. **Layer 1 overlays** in the web editor (text and title cards, arrows and shapes, blur and highlight with keyframes), sharing the model with the desktop editor. Then **B** (element lift) and **D** (brand from the page). No AI needed; every user benefits.
2. **C** (DOM privacy masks), since it's mostly extension work and becomes a reason to switch on its own.
3. **Roadmap gap #2 (frames)**, then the **Layer 2** motion doc with templates, **A** (action-timed) and **F** (self-review) built in, plus **G** (number callouts).
4. **`add_clip` for HyperFrames clips rendered locally** (small, can go any time after 1).
5. **E** (re-shoot when the UI changes), on top of `edit_video` and step-anchored edits. This is the bet nobody else can make.
6. **H**, **I**, and a hosted HyperFrames render worker, if usage asks for it.
